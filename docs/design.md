# bloop design

The one source of truth for bloop's brand, visual design, UI and UX. The system is a calm paper-and-ink one modeled on ample.computer, with opsis.app's top glass nav, tilted hero stage and list card; nothing from either site is copied (section 14). Strings marked `[V-n]` depend on an unverified product property: ship the string only once open item n (section 15) passes, otherwise ship the fallback given there. Every screen passes the checklist in section 13 before it is called done.

## 1. Brand

### Idea
bloop puts your slides on a shared screen. You scan the code on the screen, choose your file on your phone and tap Approve; it opens on the screen, and your account stays on your phone. The brand is the calm of a handover that just works: a quiet paper page, ink type, one live code. The less on the site, the better: every element earns its place.

### Name and logo
| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Logo | a dark grey lowercase "b" (#646464) over a pastel orange circle (#F4BA8B), drawn as two paths in `public/favicon.svg` (no text, no embedded image); the same file is the favicon, the nav and footer mark, and the source of the touch icon and OG image (section 9). The gray is the owner's artwork's own value (checked against it), not a rendering bug |
| Wordmark | the word `bloop` beside the logo, lowercase, Inter 600, tracking -0.03em, `--ink` |
| Name-swappable | one build constant (`SITE_NAME`) in every page, title, meta tag and string; baked only into the OG image, which a script regenerates |
| In sentences | lowercase `bloop`, even at the start of a sentence |
| Never | the logo's gray in CSS (its orange only as `--robin`, named once), a gradient on the logo, a period after the wordmark, the name inside the QR |

## 3. Voice and copy

### Rules
| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Headings, page titles and labels in Title Case with no period; no second clause in grey. | Lead with slides for lecturers; security leads only for IT. | |

| Plain beats clever: no puns, no analogy on the landing, no unexplained noun. | |

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|

### Glass
One recipe, named once (`.glass` in `base.css`; the nav pills carry the class in their markup, so `base.css` names no site selector), for every floating surface: the nav pills and their menu sheet, the viewer sidebar, its bottom sheet and handle, the viewer's control pill, the phone's sticky actions, and the components of the How It Works stills, the panels' group cards and the close card, over the warm backdrop (below), in its clearer liquid variant. Liquid, not frosted: a thin fill, a strong blur and saturation, so what passes under (the warm backdrop, the page as it scrolls) tints it, as opsis's green tints its nav. No refraction, shine or animated sheen (the loader, section 6, is the one exception); the border is the shadow's 0.5 px ring, never a CSS border. ### Why
One paragraph after the stills and before Features (section 3, Why), modeled on opsis.app's vision paragraph: the reader has seen what bloop does, and the why comes before the details. No heading: the section carries `aria-label="Why bloop"`. Centered, 600, 28 px (40 from 768), line height 1.25, tracking -0.025em, a measure of 22em, `--section` above it. Each word fills from faint (`--ink` at 50 percent on `--paper`, 3.16:1, so large text at rest low in the view still reads) to its own ink (`--robin-ink` for the name) in reading order, one word at a time, as the paragraph crosses the view: a two-stop gradient clipped to the text (`-webkit-text-fill-color: transparent`, each word an `inline-block` widened 0.025em each side as the sheen is so the clip reaches the last glyph past the tracking). The paragraph has one `view()` timeline (`--why-view`) that runs a registered number `--why` from 0 to 1 over its whole `cover` range, with `view-timeline-inset: 50% 10%` shrinking the timeline's port to the band from the view's center down to 10 percent above its bottom: the fill starts as the paragraph's top reaches the band's lower edge and ends, the last word ink, as its bottom reaches the center, so the longer scroll fills it more slowly and the word being filled ends at eye level. Why `cover` on that band and not a top-half port (`view-timeline-inset: 0 50%` with `entry`): `entry` ends when the paragraph fills the port, so a paragraph taller than half the view (a phone on its side) would finish while its lower lines were still below the center; `cover` ends at the paragraph's own bottom on the center at any size, so the fill always ends with the paragraph still in view; word `--i` of `--n` takes only its own slice, `clamp(0, --why * --n - --i, 1)`, so every word before it is ink, every word after it faint, and only one is ever partly filled (per-word timelines filled a whole line at once and read as a glitch). `site.js` hands each word its index through those two custom properties, the one style write the site's scripts make; without them the words are plain ink. Never opacity: axe reads dimmed text as low contrast, and a clipped gradient is a background it does not grade. Without `animation-timeline`, under reduced motion or in forced colors, the words are plain ink. Screen readers get one sentence: the words are spans inside an `aria-hidden` copy, and a `.sr` copy beside it holds the sentence with no word spans, only the name marked (forty inline spans can split a VoiceOver swipe on iOS), and `user-select: none`, so a selection copies the words once.

### Site build (`tools/build-pages.mjs`, `content/`)
The words of the doc pages are markdown in `content/`; the build renders them through one layout (`tools/pages/doc.html`) into `public/`, and keeps one nav, one footer and one head block on every site page.
- **Sources:** `content/privacy.md`, `terms.md`, `security.md`, `articles/*.md` (`articles/index.md` is the `/articles` page; its sections list the articles from their own frontmatter, so a title or description is written once). A file opens with flat `key: value` frontmatter (`kind`: `legal`, `article` or `index`; `title`, `description`, `order`; legal: `effective`, `version`, `numbered`, `panel`, `binds`, `summaryNote`; article: `published`, optional `updated`), then one summary paragraph, then `## Title {#id}` sections. Pages and sitemap lastmod take their dates from this frontmatter. `content/site.json` holds what is not a page's own: the origin, the one list of nav and footer links, the hand-written pages and the robots rules.
- **Markdown owns the words, the build owns every tag.** Raw HTML in content is an error. Conventions: a paragraph in a numbered document is a clause and must start with its own number (the build checks the order); `In short: ...` is an In short note; an ordered list under a clause is its (a), (b) items (in an unnumbered page, the numbered steps); `Caption: ...` before a table is its caption, and a `Status` column draws the chips (Live is on). In running text "bloop" is set in its own face and an ISO date becomes a `<time>`, by the build, never by hand (a host, an address or code is left alone). Headings stay plain text.
- **Chrome:** the nav and footer come from `tools/pages/nav.html`, `footer.html` and the link list in `site.json`, the head block (title, description, canonical, Open Graph, Twitter, structured data) from the page's own title and description. Every site page holds them between `<!-- build:nav -->`, `build:footer` and `build:seo` marker comments, including the hand-written landing and 404 (the build rewrites only between the markers, so their bodies stay theirs). A page marked `faq` in `site.json` (the landing) also holds `build:ask`, the FAQ's email line, filled from `site.email`. `{{name}}` is reserved in content prose and templates: a slot with no value fails the build, never reaches a page; to show two braces in prose, write the first as `&#123;`, while a code span or block shows them as written and is never filled. A page that draws a nav or footer outside the build fails a unit test; the other pages (`/s`, `/a`, `/code`) carry no site chrome.
- **Gate:** `npm run build:pages` writes; `npm run check:pages` (part of `npm run check`) writes nothing and fails on any file that differs or that no content file builds any more; CI rebuilds and fails on `git diff` or any new file. Generated pages begin with a `GENERATED` comment naming their source. The build is a gate, never an auto-commit. `test/unit/site-golden.test.ts` holds every preview page equal to `test/fixtures/site-preview/` (regenerated only in a commit whose message says "preview change").
- **Modes (ADR 0026):** the build emits each page twice. Preview is the site above, in `public/`. Open overlays `content/open/**` on the same documents (a draft replaces the page at its path, a new path adds one) and writes `public/open/` at the same relative paths, checked in with a `GENERATED` line and drift-checked like every other page; `build-assets` copies it to the build output's `open/` folder, which only the Worker reads (`src/worker/pages.ts`, whose variant list a unit test holds equal to this output). `site.json` `open` holds the two facts open says differently (`links`, `about`; any other key fails the build). `{{waitlist}}` in an open override or draft is the form URL from `site.json`, so no page holds a literal. An open draft whose front matter says `datePending: true` (its date is a stand-in until the final legal review stamps the real one and removes the line) is listed in `public/_launch.json`, `{"launchReady": false, "placeholder": [...]}`, built from the same `placeholderDates()` as `node tools/build-pages-cli.mjs --launch`, which prints the files and exits 1 while any remain. The datePending value must be exactly `true` and only in `content/open/`, or the build fails. The Worker answers 404 to that file, as to `/open`, and reads it through the asset binding in `launchReady(env, request)` (`src/worker/pages.ts`), which is false for a missing file, a non-200, bad JSON or anything but `launchReady: true`. A hand page differs only inside `<!-- mode:NAME -->` regions, each replaced by the same-named region of `tools/pages/open/<file>`; a region on one side only is an error, as is an override for a page with none (`index.html` has `hero-actions`, `features`, `share`, `faq-files`, `close`). The sitemap, `robots.txt` and `llms.txt` are built per mode, so preview names no open-only page, and a page's FAQ structured data is read from the variant it belongs to. The shipped HTML has no comment (`build-assets` strips them, ADR 0011). Making open permanent (ADR 0026, How to remove it) promotes `content/open/` and `tools/pages/open/` to the base pages, and deletes `public/open/`, the mode regions, `VARIANTS`, `src/worker/nav-pill.ts`'s ETag handling and the preview-only rules (`.features`, `.cell`, `.band`, `.band-tile`, the `.band::before` backdrop).
- **Structured data and the CSP:** the landing carries an `Organization` (name, url, logo, email, from `site.json`), a `WebSite` and an `FAQPage`, an article an `Article` (headline, dates, publisher), a doc page a `WebPage`, `/articles` a `CollectionPage`, as one `<script type="application/ld+json">`. It is a data block the browser never runs, so `script-src 'self'` and Trusted Types leave it alone and the CSP stays as it is (an e2e test watches the console for a violation); `<` is escaped inside it, and a unit test parses every block. The `FAQPage` is read from the landing's own FAQ markup, so it says what readers see: the build accepts only plain `<details><summary>question</summary><p>answer</p></details>` items, refuses any other item or an entity in their text, and drops a linked sentence ("See what can stay."), which says nothing in a search answer; a unit test compares it with the visible FAQ.
- **Sitemap and robots:** `public/sitemap.xml` lists the landing page and every content page, each with its content's date (the landing has none); `public/robots.txt` allows the site, disallows `/api/` and names the sitemap. The `noindex` app pages are not disallowed, so a crawler can read their tag. `public/llms.txt` (the llmstxt.org convention) gives agents the name, the landing's description, one paragraph on what bloop is (`about` in `site.json`) and every content page with its own title and description; it is in neither robots.txt nor the sitemap, which list no convention files. All three are generated, never kept by hand. Pages under `/articles` fall under the one `/*` block in `public/_headers`; a unit test keeps every page route under that block alone.
- **Articles:** `/articles` and one page each (How bloop Works, Threat Model for IT Teams, What Stays on the PC, Data and Retention), written for university IT teams and lecturers and restated from `docs/`, never copied. They use the doc layout, `legal.css` and `legal.js` as they are, so they add no size entry. Their numbers are tied to their owners by `test/unit/articles-facts.test.ts`, and each doc names the article that explains it. A title is at most 5 words like every heading, so the threat model's article is "Threat Model for IT Teams" and the PC's "What Stays on the PC". A reading time ("6 min read", 220 words a minute) sits in the header block.
- **Decisions:** `marked` (a build-time dev dependency, ADR 0001) renders the markdown: the most used parser, no dependencies of its own, and the alternative was a hand-written parser. The legal pages no longer point to GitHub; the articles carry the threat model, and `security.txt` keeps its machine-readable contact. The legal pages restart at Version 1.0 on the day the build shipped, with no archived copy. Each page's What Changed list says earlier drafts never took effect, and the old dated address `/privacy-2026-11-24` is a 301 to `/privacy` (`public/_redirects`) so a link already shared still lands; an archive page returns the day there is a version to archive. The build runs through `tools/build-pages-cli.mjs` (`sync`), which also deletes a generated page whose content file is gone.

### Loader (`core/loader.js`, one part for every loading state)
The one way bloop shows something on its way: the thumbnail, when there is one (`object-fit: cover`), else `--g1`, under the shared glass (`.glass`, so reduced transparency reaches it); a glass fill (`--glass`) that grows from the left with the bytes received (`--progress`, 0 to 1, `scaleX` over 250 ms `--ease-out`); and the landing's sheen in white light sweeping across (section 8). `role="progressbar"`, labeled "Loading {name}", `aria-valuenow` in whole percent. Where the length is unknown (no `Content-Length`, or an encoded body whose length counts other bytes) it is indeterminate: no value and no fill, the sheen alone. Reduced motion: no sweep, the fill only. Reduced transparency: the glass is `--surface` and hides the thumbnail, so the fill and the sheen take an ink tint (white on white would show no progress). Its user sizes and rounds it. The screen's stage uses it while a photo arrives (`fetch` and its stream) and, indeterminate, until a video's metadata (section 7, Video); the phone's upload row reuses it with XHR upload progress, under the row's face and over its border box, labeled "Sending {name}", with no thumbnail (the CSP has no `blob:`) and no fill while the phone prepares the file, so there are never two loaders. The photo is decoded with `createImageBitmap` (EXIF orientation applied), which every supported engine has (Safari 15.4 and later, the floor); where it fails, the photo shows its file card, as any failed photo does. A photo already drawn stays on stage when a fresh link for it arrives. Never a blur filter: a smeared picture read as a fault and said nothing about progress. Glass at rest never moves; this is the one glass that does, because it is a state, not a surface.

### Overlays (popovers and dialogs)
A tap outside an open overlay only closes it. A dialog opens modal (`showModal`), so the page under it is inert and its backdrop tap reaches nothing. A markup popover (the item sheet, the logo's question, the site menu) light-dismisses, and the platform lets that tap's click land on what lies under it, a slide's Next half or End: `core/dismiss.js`, loaded once one can open, closes it on the tap's pointerup and stops that tap's pointer events and click, and any click within 350 ms that comes before the next tap (iOS can send one after a late touchend). A tap inside any open popover, or on its invoker, is that popover's. The site menu does not load it yet: the landing's JS budget has no room. `test/unit/client-dismiss.test.ts` holds every overlay to this.

**Toast** (`core/toast.js`, `/a`): one at a time, a `.glass` card bottom center at `--inset` (where the band shows, 8 above the floating panel, whatever it holds: `phone.js` measures the panel into `--panel`, so the toast never covers the list or a button), 360 wide at most, `--r-sheet`; a title (600) and one line (`--muted`, 15). It leaves after 5 s or on a tap, and a new one replaces the old. `role="status"`, or `role="alert"` for an error; reduced motion swaps at once through the house net. A refusal the phone can name before sending (wrong type, too large, several files, a HEIC it cannot convert) is a toast and the person stays where they were; a refusal the server makes is the full "This File Can't Be Sent" page.

"Anywhere" is `--ink`, the color of the h1 and of Present, and orange bands sweep across it without a rest (`background-clip:text` over the same ink, tiled in x): a saturated `--robin-deep` band with a lighter `--robin` core, crossing at 2.5em a second, slower than the old 1 s sweep so it reads as calm and deliberate, the next band arriving before the last has gone. Why: presence without a halo; the one accent as a moving highlight, not a resting color (the old resting orange and the wash behind it read as decoration). A blurred glow muddied the letter edges, and a gradient at rest read as the gradient tell section 13 bans; under the ink between bands the letters are the h1's ink, so contrast is the h1's own there. The band runs only under `@supports` and with motion allowed; elsewhere the word is simply ink. Mid-sweep frames are not held to contrast: the band is a moving highlight.

The sheen and WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide): motion that starts by itself, lasts over 5 seconds and runs beside other content needs a way to pause it. The owner chose a continuous sheen over one that rests, so the trade-off is recorded here: it pauses off screen, in a hidden tab and while keyboard focus is in the hero (the first tab stops), hands the band to the pointer while the h1 is hovered, and never plays under reduced motion, which is the system-wide stop. That is a partial answer to 2.2.2, not a full one (a touch reader with motion on has no stop but scrolling past); the owner holds the decision.

The sheen under a hovering pointer: a frozen band read as a broken effect, so hover hands the motion to the pointer instead of stopping it (a pause is the user's control, but one with nothing to show for it looks like a fault). `public/js/sheen-follow.js`, lazy, is fetched at the first `pointerenter` of the h1 by a pointer that is not a finger (a tap keeps the handler for a later mouse), where `(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` matches; it adds no listener before that, reads the word's box once a frame only while the band moves, and reads its font size when following starts and on resize. The first move over the word eases the band from where the sweep has it to the pointer by the same ease (the shorter way round the tile), so entry never jumps; a hover never pauses the sweep, so a pointer that waits for the module sees it keep going. While following, `.sheen.is-following` has no animation and takes `background-position` from `--sheen-x`, a fraction of the 5em tile that the module writes at most once a frame from the pointer's x, clamped to the word, so a band's core (66% along the tile) comes to rest under the pointer; it is not glued to it: the x it uses trails the pointer's by an exponential ease with a 120 ms time constant (`1 - exp(-dt / tau)` per frame, so the same at any frame rate), and the loop stops once the gap is under half a pixel, so nothing runs while the pointer is still; a touch pointer never follows, and neither does a hero that holds keyboard focus (the focus pause stands); a pointer coming back during the leave ramp takes the band from where the ramp has drawn it. On `pointerleave` the band eases off from where it is shown (the eased x, not the pointer's): a 300 ms ramp from rest to the sweep's own speed (a WAAPI animation, position squared), after which the sweep restarts with a negative `animation-delay` equal to the tile offset's time, so its first frame is the last one of the ramp and nothing jumps. If the module fails to load, `site.js` marks the h1 `still` and a hovering mouse then does what keyboard focus does: the word fades to ink and the sweep stops (`@media (hover: hover)`, so a tap never leaves it on). Rule: motion never stops in one frame: a hover or focus pause of a visible animation sets `color` or `opacity` and the same rule's element transitions it (here the text fill, painted over the clipped band, fades from clear to ink; `site-pages.test.ts` guards the pairing, and that no `h1:hover` pause exists); the e2e spec in `landing.spec.ts` samples every frame across entry, leave and the focus fade.

Reduced motion (`prefers-reduced-motion:reduce`): the stage rests flat (the spring never starts); the warm backdrop is still; the sheen never plays and the word is ink like the rest of the h1; the FAQ switches instantly; the `/s` QR ring steps once a second with no glide; scene changes and a change of item swap at once; the blanket rule in `base.css` is the safety net. Nothing autoplays except the sheen and the backdrop's slow drift (40 s each way, slow enough to read as still), which are decoration, stop under reduced motion and pause off screen and in a hidden tab (one `IntersectionObserver` and `visibilitychange` in `site.js` toggle `is-paused`); the QR refresh is essential (a code must expire), so it has no pause.

## 13. Quality bar (checklist for every screen)

A screen is done when every box passes; `test/unit/site-pages.test.ts` and `test/e2e/landing.spec.ts` check the measurable ones.

- [ ] **Hero:** at most 40 words; the h1 one sentence of at most 6 words, on one line from 640 (at most two under it); the sub names the QR code, the phone and presenting in at most 2 sentences and 30 words.
- [ ] **Whole landing:** 80 to 300 words (How It Works and the close add about 30), FAQ answers included; every sentence at most 20 words; at most 4 FAQ items; every heading and label at most 5 words.
- [ ] **Try the demo** is the hero's one button, in the first view, and opens `/demo`, the close repeats it; every Waitlist link opens the Tally form and carries `data-waitlist`.
- [ ] **Nav:** one `nav` landmark, the same on every site page; the menu works with no script; the scrolled look comes from an observer, never a scroll listener.
- [ ] **Stage:** flat at rest and under reduced motion; a framed screenshot with alt text; no Source link anywhere.
- [ ] **Never-say grep:** no hit from `tools/never-say.txt` in any page or string; no nothing-stays, no sign-in-is-gone, no runs-everywhere, no freshness claim.
- [ ] **Mobile (390):** no horizontal scroll, targets at least 44 px.
- [ ] **Contrast:** WCAG AA on every text and control; axe and Lighthouse accessibility 1.0 in CI.
- [ ] **Budgets:** section 11.
- [ ] **No tells:** no gradient, glow or glossy button, except the robin accents (the "Anywhere" sheen and the warm backdrop under the stills and the close panel, sections 5, 6 and 8); no eyebrow or mono kicker (the match number is not a kicker); no two-tone headline; no status dots or tinted icons; glass only on the surfaces section 4 lists, in its one recipe; no fake logos, testimonials or counts; no em or en dashes, exclamation marks or emoji.

### Coherence
One of each: type scale (600 and 400), card (flat white on paper), button shape (the pill in three roles), radius logic, shadow recipe (the stage frame, the QR card, glass), motion language, icon style (Lucide, stroke 2, only where it carries meaning), product pictures as real HTML fragments of the app in a `--paper` still. One voice (section 3); the same sentence for the same claim on every page.
